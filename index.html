Six meals, six cities, one country

Mexico is our heritage. We have no family there to visit, so we found it at the table instead. These are six we did not want to leave.

The six meals

  1. 01

    Tijuana, Baja California

    Venue: Off Avenida Revolución

    Taco estilo Tijuana

    Time at the table: 1h 00m

    Photograph pending

    Salt and pepper on top sirloin, chopped fine over charcoal. Two small corn tortillas, never flour. It arrives already dressed, so there is no salsa bar and no decision to make.

    Fact

    The beef comes up from Ciudad Obregón, Sonora. Sonora raises it, Tijuana changes the wrapper.

    Note

    I gave my wife a taco tour for Christmas. December 23rd, four hours on foot, and of everything we ate that day this is the one I still think about.

  2. 02

    Rosarito, Baja California

    Venue: Tacos El Yaqui

    Tacos perrones

    Time at the table: 2h 00m

    Photograph pending

    Tijuana says corn, never flour. Forty minutes down the coast the perrón reverses all of it: a flour tortilla, arrachera instead of chopped sirloin, beans and guacamole built in. Same coast, almost the opposite taco.

    Fact

    Tacos El Yaqui opened in Rosarito in February 1984, at Mar del Norte and La Palma. They say the name came from a customer who took a bite and said “este taco sí está bien perrón.”

    Note

    The line was an hour long and they sell micheladas while you wait in it. Sobremesa started before the food did.

  3. 03

    Ensenada and Valle de Guadalupe, Baja California

    Venue: El Trailero

    Taco de pescado

    Time at the table: 1h 00m

    Photograph pending

    Battered fish, cabbage, crema, lime. Codified in Ensenada in the early 1960s by vendors working the Mercado Negro. Several claimants, no single inventor, which is what a real street food usually looks like.

    Fact

    Forty minutes inland, Valle de Guadalupe grows roughly 70% of Mexico's wine. The modern industry starts with Casa Pedro Domecq in 1972.

    Note

    El Trailero started as a truck stop. We still go there first, every time, before we drive up to the valley.

  4. 04

    Guadalajara, Jalisco

    Venue: La Chata

    Torta ahogada

    Time at the table: 1h 30m

    Photograph pending

    A pork sandwich drowned, not drizzled, in chile de árbol salsa. The whole dish rests on the bread. Birote salado is a salt-forward sourdough with a crust built to be submerged and still hold.

    Fact

    Jalisco producers have pursued a denominación de origen for birote, the same protection tequila has.

    Note

    The cazuela at La Chata is a drink, not a dish, and it is my wife's order before she looks at the menu. We liked the place enough to go to the one in Puerto Vallarta too.

  5. 05

    Ciudad de México

    Venue: El Huequito

    Tacos al pastor

    Time at the table: 2h 00m

    Photograph pending

    Levantine immigrants brought vertical spit roasting to Puebla in the 1930s. Mexico City swapped lamb for pork, added chile adobo and a corn tortilla, and by the 1960s had made it the city's signature.

    Fact

    El Huequito opened in 1959, El Tizoncito in 1966. Both claim it. Neither claim is settled.

    Note

    We have tried El Tizoncito and Orinoco. We keep going back to El Huequito, under the freeway bridge, our first and still our favorite.

  6. 06

    Oaxaca de Juárez, Oaxaca

    Venue: A street stand at Plaza Santo Domingo

    Tlayuda

    Time at the table: 2h 00m

    Photograph pending

    A tlayuda is not a dish, it is a stage. The word names a large thin tortilla, semi-toasted and partly dried so it keeps for weeks before anyone crisps it. Asiento, black beans, quesillo, cabbage, avocado, tasajo.

    Fact

    The tortilla runs 30 to 40 centimeters across, made from bolita corn from the Valles Centrales, chosen because it holds structure at that diameter.

    Note

    We went during Día de los Muertos. The templo was so packed we could barely move, and that was part of it.
